WHAT IS SALAH?
Prayer is one of the 5 pillars of Islam

Muslims pray five times a day: Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha. It is how we stay connected to Allah every day. You do not need any experience. Go through the five parts below in order, one at a time, at your own pace. Each part is short and has pictures.

A SHORT HISTORY
A PILLAR OF ISLAM
Salah is the second pillar of Islam, after the declaration of faith.
THE GIFT OF THE NIGHT JOURNEY
The five daily prayers were prescribed during the Night Journey (Isra and Mi'raj), before the Hijrah. Fifty prayers were reduced to five, with the reward of fifty.
HOW IT REACHED US
The Prophet (peace be upon him) said, “Pray as you have seen me pray” (Bukhari). His Companions described his prayer in detail, and it was passed down through every generation.
FIRST TO BE ASKED
The Prophet said the first thing a servant is asked about on the Day of Judgement is the prayer (Tirmidhi, Abu Dawud).

Fajr has 2 rak‘ahs, from true dawn until sunrise. Zuhr has 4, from when the sun passes its highest point. ‘Asr has 4, from when an object’s shadow equals its height until sunset. Osoul adds that you can know the time from a reliable watch or by hearing the adhan. Some scholars differ on small details, so ask your local mosque.

Women are not required to pray the obligatory prayers in congregation, but may attend the mosque: the Prophet ﷺ said not to prevent them from the mosques, though their homes are better for them (Abu Dawud, as cited in that book). Zarabozo encourages new Muslims, men and women, to attend when they can.

Osoul says prayer is valid in any pure place, such as a house, a street or the wilderness. The condition is that your body, clothes and place are free of impurity. Fiqh us-Sunnah relates that the Prophet ﷺ prayed sometimes barefoot and sometimes in shoes, and quotes the advice to check your shoes and rub off any dirt on the ground before praying in them. So clean shoes are permitted in these accounts; if yours may carry dirt, check them first.

A traveller may shorten the four-rak‘ah prayers (Zuhr, ‘Asr and ‘Isha) to two, while Fajr and Maghrib stay the same. Fiqh us-Sunnah quotes the Prophet ﷺ calling this “a charity that Allah, the Exalted, has bestowed upon you, so accept His charity”, and records that scholars differ over whether shortening is obligatory, a stressed sunnah or only preferred, and over how far and how long one must travel. A traveller may also combine Zuhr with ‘Asr, and Maghrib with ‘Isha, in either one’s time. Zarabozo warns against combining out of laziness.

Fiqh us-Sunnah says calmness means staying until your bones settle. The adhan is the call announcing that the time of a prayer has begun; the iqamah follows when the prayer is about to start. Scholars say it is a communal obligation, and the prayer is valid without it. Fiqh us-Sunnah says to repeat after the caller, except at “Hayya ‘ala as-salah” and “Hayya ‘ala al-falah”, where you say “La hawla wa la quwwata illa billah”. The book stresses doing this gently, with a cheerful face, praise and small gifts. Before seven, teach cleanliness, wudu, Al-Fatihah and short surahs, let them stand beside you in prayer, and do not demand all five at once. Parents should lead by example. Prayer is the second pillar of Islam and, as Osoul puts it, the greatest connection between a servant and his Lord. Allah says prayer “restrains from immorality and wrongdoing” (Quran 29:45). The Prophet ﷺ likened the five prayers to bathing five times a day in a river at your door (Muslim), and another hadith (Muslim) says the sins between the prayers are wiped off if major sins are avoided. Prayer also brings calm: when the Prophet ﷺ faced a serious matter he hurried to prayer (Ahmad and Abu Dawud, as cited). It is the first deed we will be asked about.

Sources: Osoul Center, How to Pray: An Illustrated Explanation; Before You Pray, Anas al-Gawz (Dar-us-Salam); Why do we Pray? (two Friday sermons); Our Children and Prayer (Al-Falah Foundation translation)

Osoul lists what breaks the prayer: failing a condition when able, deliberately leaving a pillar or required act, deliberate unnecessary movement, speaking or walking, laughing, and deliberately eating or drinking. Fiqh us-Sunnah notes that speaking in ignorance of the rule did not invalidate a man’s prayer, and that scholars differ on how many movements count as “many”. Disliked but not invalidating: turning your face, fidgeting, looking at the sky, praying while needing the toilet or with food served, and facing distractions.

Sources: Osoul Center, How to Pray: An Illustrated Explanation (invalidators; disliked things); Fiqh us-Sunnah, Sayyid Sabiq (chapters on the prayer) (what invalidates the prayer)

Conditions come before the prayer, like wudu, facing the Qiblah and the time. Pillars, such as standing if able, the opening takbir, Al-Fatihah, bowing, prostration and the final salaam, must be done, or the prayer is invalid even if forgotten. Required acts, such as the other takbirs, invalidate the prayer only if left on purpose; if forgotten, you make the two prostrations of forgetfulness. Sunnah acts, like the opening supplication, add reward. Scholars differ on some items: the treatise counts blessings on the Prophet ﷺ as a pillar, but Fiqh us-Sunnah calls them preferred, and says most scholars hold the first tashahhud is sunnah.

Sources: The Conditions, Pillars and Requirements of the Prayer, Imam Muhammad ibn ‘Abd al-Wahhab (English translation); Fiqh us-Sunnah, Sayyid Sabiq (chapters on the prayer) (obligatory and sunnah acts of prayer); Osoul Center, How to Pray: An Illustrated Explanation

Before You Pray

ط
PART 1 OF 5 · GET READY
Six things to check
Take a moment to get ready. It only takes a minute.
1. Be in Wudhu

Make Wudhu before you begin. If you are not sure how, learn it first.

2. The time has begun

Each prayer has its own window. Pray inside it, and try to pray early.

3. Clean body, clothes and place

Your body, clothes and the place where you pray should be clean.

4. Cover properly

Dress modestly. Men cover at least from the navel to the knees; women cover everything except the face and hands, according to most scholars.

5. Face the Qibla

Turn toward the Kaaba in Makkah. Use the Qibla compass if you are unsure.

6. Make your intention

Decide in your heart which prayer you are praying. The intention is in the heart, not said aloud.

Sources
Source: Quran 4:103, 5:6, 2:144 and 7:31; Sahih al-Bukhari 1

How the rak'ahs flow
2 rak'ahs (Fajr): Rak'ah 1, then Rak'ah 2, then sit for the Tashahhud, Salawat, dua and Salam.
3 rak'ahs (Maghrib): After the 2nd rak'ah sit for the Tashahhud only, then stand for the 3rd, and finish with the full closing sitting.
4 rak'ahs: Same as above, with a 4th rak'ah before the closing sitting.
Good to know

These are the obligatory (fard) prayers. Extra sunnah prayers and Witr vary between schools, so follow your own school for those.

Source: Quran 4:103, 11:114 and 17:78; Sahih Muslim 612

Common Mistakes

خ
PART 5 OF 5 · AVOID SLIPS
Seven easy slips
Small habits that are simple to fix, even for experienced worshippers.
1
Rushing

Moving quickly between positions. Be still and calm in each one before moving on.

Source: Sahih al-Bukhari 757
2
Looking up or around

Keep your eyes on the place of prostration and do not look around.

Source: Sahih al-Bukhari 750
3
Bending the back unevenly

In ruku keep your back and head level, and stand fully straight afterwards.

Source: Sahih Muslim 397
4
Fidgeting

Avoid unnecessary movement such as playing with clothes or looking at the time.

Source: Quran 23:1–2
5
Skipping the sitting

Do not skip the short sitting between the two prostrations.

Source: Sahih al-Bukhari 757
6
Forgetting Al-Fatiha

It is recited in every rak'ah. Learn it first.

Source: Sahih al-Bukhari 756
7
Praying after the time

Pray inside the window of each prayer, and do not delay without reason.

Source: Quran 4:103

Jumu'ah

ج
JUMU'AH
The Friday prayer
The weekly congregational prayer, the best day of the week.
What it is

Jumu'ah replaces Dhuhr on Friday for those who are required to attend. There is a sermon (khutbah), followed by a two-rak'ah prayer prayed together in congregation.

How to prepare
1
Take a bath and wear clean clothes
Sahih al-Bukhari 877

Dua Qunut (Witr, 3rd rak'ah)
اللَّهُمَّ إِنَّا نَسْتَعِينُكَ وَنَسْتَغْفِرُكَ وَنُؤْمِنُ بِكَ وَنَتَوَكَّلُ عَلَيْكَ وَنُثْنِي عَلَيْكَ الْخَيْرَ وَنَشْكُرُكَ وَلَا نَكْفُرُكَ وَنَخْلَعُ وَنَتْرُكُ مَنْ يَفْجُرُكَ اللَّهُمَّ إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَلَكَ نُصَلِّي وَنَسْجُدُ وَإِلَيْكَ نَسْعَى وَنَحْفِدُ وَنَرْجُو رَحْمَتَكَ وَنَخْشَى عَذَابَكَ إِنَّ عَذَابَكَ بِالْكُفَّارِ مُلْحِقٌ
Say it: Allahumma inna nasta'inuka wa nastaghfiruka wa nu'minu bika wa natawakkalu alayka wa nuthni alaykal-khayr, wa nashkuruka wa la nakfuruka, wa nakhla'u wa natruku man yafjuruk. Allahumma iyyaka na'budu wa laka nusalli wa nasjud, wa ilayka nas'a wa nahfid, wa narju rahmataka wa nakhsha adhabak, inna adhabaka bil-kuffari mulhiq.

Meaning: O Allah, we ask You for help and forgiveness, we believe in You and rely on You, and we praise You with all good. We thank You and do not deny You, and we leave and abandon the one who disobeys You. O Allah, You alone we worship, to You we pray and prostrate, and toward You we strive. We hope for Your mercy and fear Your punishment, for Your punishment will surely reach the disbelievers.

Note: Commonly recited in the Hanafi school. Other schools may recite a different Qunut. Ask a scholar.
